CapyDrop Privacy Policy

Publisher: RADiation Team
Contact: radiationsupport@gmail.com
Effective date: 3 October 2026
Applies to: CapyDrop for Android (package com.capycorp.capydrop)

The short version for parents

  • CapyDrop has no account, no sign-in, no chat and no way to play or talk with other people.
  • We, RADiation Team, do not run any servers for the game. The game's own code sends nothing to us. Progress, settings and the game's technical logs stay on the device.
  • The game shows ads from Google AdMob. Every ad request, for every player, is marked as directed at children and limited to G-rated content. Google's ad software still receives some technical information from the device; this policy explains what.
  • On the first launch the game asks the player's age once. The answer stays on the device and decides only whether the game shows buttons that lead out of the game.
  • The game offers optional purchases: "No ads", which turns off the banner and the full-screen ads, and packs of coins and crystals. Nothing has to be bought to play. Google Play takes and confirms every payment (on a child's account, through Family Link); the game never sees card details. Google Play restores "No ads" on the same Google account by itself.
  • The only permission the game asks for while running is permission to show notifications (on Android 13 and newer).

Who we are and what this policy covers

CapyDrop is published by RADiation Team ("we", "us"). This policy explains what information the game handles, where that information goes and what you can do about it. It is written for parents and guardians, because CapyDrop is made for children aged three and up as well as for older players.

You can reach us at radiationsupport@gmail.com.

The age question

When the game is started for the first time, it asks one question before showing the Terms of Use: "How old are you?". The answer is a button: one for each age from 3 to 17, and one for 18+. All the buttons look the same, none is selected in advance, and the question has to be answered before the game continues.

  • Where it is kept. The answer is written into the game's save file on the device.
  • What it is used for. Only to decide whether the game shows its own buttons that lead out of the game. A player who answered an age under 13 is not shown the Share button or any link that leaves the game, except the link to this Privacy Policy, which is open to everyone.
  • What it does not change. Ads are requested in the child-directed mode for every player, whatever the answer (see "Advertising"). The answer does not unlock anything else, and it does not change the purchases: Google Play confirms every payment, whatever the answer (see "Purchases").
  • Who receives it. No one. The game does not send the answer to us, to Google or to any ad network, and does not write it into its event log or error log.
  • When it leaves the device. Only together with the save file: when you copy the save yourself with "Export save", or when Android's own backup copies the game's files (see "Android backup and moving to a new phone").
  • Changing the answer. There is no button in the game to change it. Clearing the game's storage in Android settings removes it, and the game asks again on the next launch. Uninstalling the game removes it too, but if Android backup is on, reinstalling can bring the old save back, answer included, and then the question is not asked. Importing a save replaces the answer with the one stored in that save.

Information kept on the device

Progress and settings

The game keeps a save file, a backup copy of it and a temporary file used while saving, in the game's own storage area on the device. The save holds:

  • game progress: scores, coins and crystals, the town and its buildings, quests, achievements, arena characters and their upgrades, mini-game progress, the daily calendar and game timestamps;
  • settings: sound and music volume, vibration, screen shake, calm mode, large text, language and graphics quality;
  • the age answer;
  • which version of the Terms of Use was accepted, whether it included the clause about purchases, and whether the game has already asked for the notification permission;
  • counters that pace the ads, such as how many rounds have ended since the last full-screen ad and when a rewarded video was last watched;
  • purchase records: Google Play's identifier (purchase token) of each purchase already credited, so that no purchase is ever credited twice, whether a purchase is still being completed, for each pack the player chose to buy in the last seven days the amount its card showed, and whether "No ads" is active on this device.

The save carries a signature that lets the game notice if the file was changed outside the game. This is not encryption: the file is plain text. A few small interface notes, such as whether a tip has already been shown, are kept separately in the game's preferences. The game picks its language from the device's language on the first launch; you can change it in Settings.

The game's event log

The game writes a local event log (analytics.ndjson). Each entry has the time and the name of an event, with game details such as level, coins, scores and settings. Events include a session start, a finished round, tutorial steps, accepting or declining the Terms of Use, whether an ad was offered, shown or completed, the steps of a purchase (the product chosen and how the purchase ended), and some events in the town, the arena and the mini-games. It also notes technical events: the type of an error and the place in the program where it happened, error counts and missing sounds. One mini-game also tags its events with a random number created at each launch.

The log contains no name, no contact details, no location and no device or advertising identifier. It stays on the device: the game does not send it to us or to anyone else. When it reaches 512 KB, it is moved aside and a new log is started; only one previous copy is kept.

The game's error log

The game also keeps a local error log (capydrop_errors.log) to help find a problem a parent reports to us. Each time the game starts, it writes one line with the time (UTC), the game version, the device model, the Android version, the amount of memory, the number of processor cores, the graphics chip and graphics system, and the graphics quality level. When something goes wrong, it records the time, the type of the error, its message (up to 600 characters) and the place in the program where it happened (up to 1,800 characters), at most 60 entries per session. It also notes missing sounds and low-memory warnings.

The error log stays on the device; the game never sends it anywhere. When it reaches 256 KB, it is moved aside and a new one is started; only one previous copy is kept. A parent can choose to copy it and send it to us (see "Things you choose to do").

Google's consent record

Google's consent tool (the User Messaging Platform, described below) keeps its own record of privacy choices on the device.

Advertising

CapyDrop is free and shows ads from Google AdMob:

  • a banner at the bottom of the home screen, shown only after the first lesson;
  • a full-screen ad after some finished rounds;
  • rewarded videos that start only when the player taps to watch one for an in-game reward.

After the "No ads" purchase. The banner and the full-screen ads are no longer requested or shown. Rewarded videos stay available and still start only when the player taps for one, so Google's ad software stays in the game and everything below still applies to it. See "Purchases".

Settings for children, for every player. Every ad request is marked for child-directed treatment and limited to the G (general audiences) content rating. Google states that child-directed requests do not receive personalised (interest-based) ads or remarketing. These settings limit how ads are chosen; they do not mean that Google's ad software processes no information.

Advertising ID. The game does not declare the Google Play services advertising-ID permission (AD_ID); on Android 13 and newer this means the game cannot read the advertising ID. The Android Privacy Sandbox permissions for ad ID, ad attribution and ad topics are removed as well.

What Google's ad software processes. Like any app that shows AdMob ads, the game includes Google's Mobile Ads software. According to Google, it processes the device's IP address, the approximate location derived from it, interactions with ads and the app, diagnostic information and applicable device or app identifiers (for example the app set ID, which can still be used when the advertising ID is not available), for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. It also sends its own crash reports to Google. Google describes this in its data disclosure for the Mobile Ads SDK and its notes on crash reporting. Google handles this information under the Google Privacy Policy; see also how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Privacy choices. Before ads are requested, the game asks Google's User Messaging Platform whether a privacy or consent message is needed where the player is (for example in the European Economic Area or the UK). This request is marked as coming from a user under the age of consent. Where a message is required, Google shows it. When Google requires a way to change the choice later, a "Privacy options" row appears in the game's Settings.

Tapping an ad. Ads are chosen and shown by Google. Tapping an ad can open Google Play or a website; this is outside the game's own age rule, which covers the game's own buttons.

Before the Terms of Use are accepted. The game's own code does not start the ads, the consent check, the reminders or the connection to Google Play's purchases until a grown-up taps "I ACCEPT" on the Terms of Use. Google's ad library also contains a start-up component that Android runs when the app opens; what it does at that moment is decided by Google.

The game does not include any other analytics or crash-reporting service of its own (such as Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics or Unity Analytics).

Purchases

CapyDrop can be played in full without buying anything. It offers these optional purchases, sold through Google Play:

  • No ads, bought once: the banner and the full-screen ads are no longer shown on the Google account that bought it. Rewarded videos stay and start only when the player taps for one.
  • Packs of crystals (three sizes) and packs of coins (two sizes), which are used up when they are added to the game.

The shop opens only when the player taps the "+" next to the coins or the crystals, or "Remove ads" in Settings; the game does not interrupt play with offers. Prices are set in Google Play: the shop shows the price Google Play gives it, in the local currency, and Google Play's own purchase window shows it again before anything is paid. Where Google Play does not sell, the shop is not shown.

Who takes the payment. Google Play. Every payment is confirmed in Google Play's own window, with the checks set on the Google account (for example its password), and on a child's account supervised with Family Link with a parent's approval when the parent has turned approvals on. Card and bank details, and the name and email address of the Google account, stay with Google and never reach the game.

What the game receives from Google Play. The products and their prices for the shop, and for each purchase Google Play's receipt: the order number, the product, the time of purchase, its state (for example paid, waiting for a parent's approval, or cancelled) and a purchase token, which identifies the purchase and which the game hands back to Google Play to complete it. The game keeps the purchase tokens and whether "No ads" is active in the save on the device (see "Progress and settings") and writes the purchase steps into its local event log. It sends none of this to us.

How "No ads" comes back. Google Play keeps the "No ads" purchase on the Google account. Each time the game starts (once the Terms of Use are accepted), comes back to the screen or opens the shop, it asks Google Play which purchases the account holds. That is how "No ads" returns after a reinstall, or on another phone signed in to the same Google account. If Google Play answers that the account no longer holds it (for example after a refund, or on a different Google account), the ads come back. If Google Play cannot be reached, the game keeps what it last knew, so a missing connection never brings the ads back. Bought coins and crystals are not kept by Google Play: they are part of the save on the device, like the rest of the progress.

The software. The purchases use Google Play Billing 9.0.0 through Unity In-App Purchasing 5.4.3, which comes with Unity services core 1.18.0. The game does not start Unity's online services (Unity Gaming Services), which that library would need before it could report anything to Unity; the purchase itself is handled by Google Play under the Google Privacy Policy and the Google Play Terms of Service.

What we receive as the seller. Google Play gives us, in its developer console, its own records of the orders: for example the order number, the product, the price and taxes, the date, and where the purchase was made (the country and, where Google provides them, the region, city or postal code). We use them only for accounting, refunds and questions about a purchase. They are not linked to anything in the game, and the game cannot tell us who bought.

Refunds. Refunds follow Google Play's refund policies. You can ask Google Play for a refund, or write to us with the order number (it starts with "GPA.").

What we, RADiation Team, receive

Nothing from the game. The game has no server of ours to talk to, and none of the files described above is sent to us. If you buy something, Google Play gives us its own record of the order (see "Purchases"). Apart from that, we receive information only when you choose to write to us, for example an email or an error log you paste into a message. We use it only to answer you, and we do not share it or add you to any mailing list.

If you are in the EEA or the UK. Our legal basis for using what you send us to answer you is our legitimate interest in answering questions about our game (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). For Google Play's order records, it is the purchase itself and our legal duty to keep accounts (GDPR Article 6(1)(b) and (c)). This is the only personal information we receive. Google decides how its ad software uses the information it receives, and on what legal basis, as the Google Privacy Policy explains. Before any ad is requested, the game asks Google's consent tool whether a privacy message is needed (see "Privacy choices" above); choices made in a message that Google shows apply to Google's processing.

Permissions

These are the Android permissions in the game, in plain words. The only one the game ever asks for on screen is notifications.

  • Internet and network state: needed by Google's ad software to load ads.
  • Show notifications: for the game's reminders. On Android 13 and newer, the game asks once, after the first lesson. On older Android versions notifications are allowed by default. You can turn them off at any time in Android settings.
  • Run at start-up: so that reminders already scheduled are set again after the phone restarts.
  • Vibration: for short vibrations during play. They can be switched off in the game's Settings.
  • Keep the device awake and run a foreground service: added by Google's ad software for its background task scheduler (Android WorkManager).
  • An internal permission named after the game: used only inside the game, so that no other app can send it messages.
  • Google Play Billing (com.android.vending.BILLING): lets the game open Google Play's purchase window. It gives the game no access to payment details.

The game does not request location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage, Wi-Fi or local-network permissions, and it does not request the advertising-ID permission. Google's ad software also lists some kinds of apps it may open when an ad is tapped (a web browser, Google Play, a calendar, messages or the phone dialer), and Google Play Billing names the Google Play Store app it connects to; these are not permissions.

Reminders

The game sets local reminders on the device itself; there is no push-message service. At most three reminders are set, each between 09:00 and 20:00 device time, only when the game goes into the background and only after the Terms of Use have been accepted. They are cancelled when the game is opened again. They look like: "The office hit its earning cap!", "Today's gift is waiting" and "The arena is open!". In Android settings they appear under the channel "Rewards and reminders", where you can turn them off.

Things you choose to do

These actions happen only when someone taps them. Apart from a purchase, which goes to Google Play, the game itself does not send anything anywhere.

  • Buy (the "+" next to the coins or the crystals, or Settings → Remove ads): opens Google Play's own purchase window (see "Purchases").
  • Export save (Settings → For parents): copies the whole save file, including the age answer, to the device clipboard as text.
  • Import save (Settings → For parents): after a confirmation, reads a save from the clipboard and replaces the current progress. The logs are not changed.
  • Copy error log (Settings → For parents): copies up to the last 16,000 characters of the error log to the clipboard, so you can paste it into a message to us.
  • Share (players aged 13 and over): the round details window has a Share button that sends a short line of text with the game's name and the points scored to the app you pick in Android's share menu. It is hidden for players under 13.
  • Privacy Policy link (the Terms of Use screen and Settings): opens this policy in the browser over HTTPS. It is the only link among the game's own buttons that leads out of the game in this version, and it is open to every player.

Anything you paste or send to another app is handled by that app and by the people you send it to.

No accounts, chat or multiplayer

This version of CapyDrop has no game account, no sign-in, no Google Play Games, no cloud save, no chat and no multiplayer. It does not look for other devices on your network. Purchases need no game account either: Google Play keeps "No ads" on the Google account that is signed in to Google Play.

Coins, crystals and other game items, bought or earned, cannot be exchanged for money. If a later version offers any of the features above, we will update this policy before that version is released.

Android backup and moving to a new phone

CapyDrop does not switch off Android's backup. If backup is turned on for the device, Android may copy the game's files, including the save (with the age answer, the bought coins and crystals and the purchase records), the settings and the logs, to the Google account's backup, and may restore them after a reinstall or when moving to a new phone. This backup is run by Android and Google under the device's backup settings; we cannot see it. You can turn backup off in Android settings. "No ads" does not depend on this backup: Google Play restores it (see "Purchases").

Security

Google states that the information its ad software collects is sent over encrypted connections (TLS). The save and the logs are kept in the game's folder on the device's storage (Android/data/com.capycorp.capydrop/files). Android 11 and newer keeps other apps out of that folder; on Android 10 and older, an app that has been given access to the phone's storage may be able to read it. A computer connected by cable to an unlocked phone may also be able to open it. The game does not encrypt these files itself. Payments take place in Google Play's own window; the game never handles card details.

Keeping and deleting information

  • On the device. Progress and settings stay until they are replaced or removed. The purchase identifiers stay as long as the save does, so that a purchase is never credited twice. The logs are limited by size, as described above, not by a number of days.
  • No in-game delete button. The game has no control that erases everything. To delete all of the game's information on the device, open Android Settings → Apps → CapyDrop → Storage & cache (on some phones, Storage) and clear the storage, or uninstall the game. After the storage is cleared, the game asks the age again; after a reinstall, Android backup may bring the old files back (see "Android backup and moving to a new phone"). Clearing the storage also removes bought coins and crystals; it does not cancel "No ads", which Google Play keeps on the account.
  • Copies elsewhere. Android backups, and anything you exported, copied or sent, stay where they are until you delete them there.
  • With us. We hold no data about players on any server. Google Play's order records stay in its developer console under Google's rules, and we keep what accounting and tax law require us to keep, for as long as it requires. Emails you send us are kept only as long as we need them to deal with your message.
  • With Google. Google keeps and deletes the information its services receive under its own retention policy. Your purchases are listed in your Google Play order history. Removing CapyDrop does not by itself ask Google to delete anything.

Children's privacy

CapyDrop is made for children aged three and up and for families. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children:

  • the game does not ask a child for a name, email address, phone number, photo, voice or location, and there is nothing in the game where a child can write to other people or share pictures;
  • the only question asked is the player's age, as a number, and the answer stays on the device;
  • every ad request is child-directed and G-rated for every player, whatever the age answer; this is the setting Google provides for apps covered by the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), and it turns off personalised ads and remarketing;
  • every privacy-choice request is marked as coming from a user under the age of consent, the setting Google provides for the EU and UK GDPR;
  • ads come only from Google AdMob, which is on Google Play's list of ad software self-certified for apps that serve children;
  • the advertising ID is not requested, and the game has no analytics service of its own that leaves the device;
  • purchases are optional and never needed to play, the game does not interrupt play with offers, and Google Play confirms every payment; a parent can require approval for every purchase (see "Your choices and rights as a parent");
  • players under 13 are not shown links out of the game or the Share button, except the link to this policy.

If you believe a child has sent us personal information, for example in an email, contact us and we will delete it.

Your choices and rights as a parent

Because the game sends us nothing, we hold no personal information about your child on our servers. The information described in this policy is on the device in your hands, and you control it:

  • See it: "Export save" shows the save as text; "Copy error log" shows the error log.
  • Delete it: clear the game's storage or uninstall the game.
  • Stop reminders: turn off the game's notifications in Android settings.
  • Ad privacy choices: where Google requires them, use "Privacy options" in the game's Settings.
  • Purchases: in the Google Play app's settings, ask for authentication for all purchases; on a child's account, turn on purchase approvals in Family Link. Your orders, and the way to ask for a refund, are in your Google Play order history.
  • Backup: turn Android backup on or off in Android settings.

You can write to us at radiationsupport@gmail.com with any question about this policy, or to ask what information we hold, to have it corrected or deleted, or to object to how it is used. This includes anything you have sent us by email. Please do not send a child's full name, home address, photos or identity documents. Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority. For information processed by Google, Google is responsible; you can use the tools described in the Google Privacy Policy.

Changes to this policy

We will update this policy when the way the game handles information changes, for example before a version adds multiplayer, a new kind of purchase or a new service. The effective date at the top shows which version applies. If a change also affects the Terms of Use, the game will ask a grown-up to accept them again.
