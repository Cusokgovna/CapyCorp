FalseAlibi Privacy Policy

Publisher: RADiation Team
Contact: radiationsupport@gmail.com
Effective date: 5 November 2026
Applies to: FalseAlibi, the online detective game for computers, sold on Steam

The short version

  • FalseAlibi has no account with us. We, RADiation Team, run no servers of our own, and the game sends nothing to us directly.
  • To play online together, the game uses Unity Gaming Services (sign-in without a password, lobbies, the relay that carries the match between players) and Vivox (voice chat) from Unity Technologies, and Steam from Valve for your name and friends' invitations.
  • Your voice goes only to the players near your character, live. We do not record voice chat. You can switch voice off, mute your microphone, use push-to-talk, and mute any other player.
  • Your notebook stays on your computer. Only the pages you tear out and pin to the shared board are seen by the other players of that match.
  • No ads, no advertising IDs, no analytics or tracking tools, no purchases inside the game. We do not sell or share your information.
  • The Unity engine may send anonymous crash and error reports so that we can fix problems.

Who we are and what this policy covers

RADiation Team makes and publishes FalseAlibi. This policy explains what information the game uses, where it goes and what you can do about it. It covers the game itself. Steam, Unity and Vivox are separate services with their own policies, linked at the end.

Information kept on your computer

  • Settings: graphics, sound, controls, language, your display name if you typed one, and your choices about voice.
  • Your mute list: the players you have muted, by their anonymous player IDs. It never leaves your computer, so nobody learns whom you muted.
  • Your notebook: the text of your notes is never sent anywhere. A page you tear out and pin to the shared board becomes part of that match: the other players of the match see it, and it is part of the match's Reconstruction.
  • Saved match records: when you press "Save the record" after a match, the game saves the Reconstruction (the case, the players' display names and what they did, the pages on the board) as a file. The file stays on your computer unless you share it yourself. It contains no voices.
  • A technical log (Player.log): errors and connection messages, including your anonymous player ID. It is not uploaded; the main menu's "Log folder" button opens its folder.

All of this is in %USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\FalseAlibi\FalseAlibi (settings also in the Windows registry under HKEY_CURRENT_USER\Software\FalseAlibi\FalseAlibi).

Steam (Valve)

When you start the game through Steam, it reads your Steam ID, your Steam display name and your Steam language, to show your name to other players (until you choose another one), to let your Steam friends see that you are playing and join you with an invitation, and to pick the game's language. While you are in an online game, your Steam friends can see its join code in your Steam status. Valve handles your Steam information under the Steam Privacy Policy.

Online play: Unity Gaming Services

  • Sign-in (Unity Authentication). The game signs you in anonymously, without a password, and Unity gives you a random player ID. Unity receives your IP address and basic information about your computer to do this.
  • Lobbies (Unity Lobby). A game you create or join is a lobby on Unity's servers: its join code, the map, the case language, and the display names of the players in it. If the host makes the game open, its host name, map and number of players are shown in other players' list of open games. A lobby is deleted when the game ends.
  • Relay (Unity Relay). Unity's relay servers carry the match between the players' computers. They see your IP address; the other players do not.
  • The host. The player who hosts a game receives the other players' display names and anonymous player IDs; the IDs let the host keep a removed player out of that game.

Voice chat: Vivox

Voice chat uses Vivox by Unity Technologies. While voice is on, your microphone is streamed through Vivox servers to the players whose characters are near yours. Vivox processes your anonymous player ID, a shortened IP address and information about your device. We do not record voice chat. In the pause menu you can turn voice chat off, mute your own microphone, use push-to-talk, and change the voices' volume; in the list of players you can mute any player for yourself.

Voices in the Reconstruction (only if you agree)

After the verdict the game can replay the match, and with your consent your voice from the match too. The switch "My voice in the match replay" in the pause menu is off unless you turn it on. If it is on, at the end of the match your recorded voice is sent to the host's game and played back to the players of that match. It is kept only in memory and is never written to a file, not even to a saved match record.

Crash and error reports

The Unity engine may send reports of crashes and errors to Unity: what went wrong, the game's version, and your device's model, operating system, graphics card and memory. The reports do not contain your name, your notes or your voice. We read them in Unity's dashboard to fix problems.

What we, RADiation Team, receive

We have no servers of our own and receive nothing from the game directly. Through Unity's dashboard we can see how much the online services are used, the anonymous player IDs of players who signed in, and crash reports. From Valve we receive sales and play statistics that do not identify you. If you write to us, we receive your email and whatever you choose to tell us.

What we do not do

  • No ads and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics, tracking or profiling tools.
  • No purchases inside the game.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your information with anyone except the services above, which handle it to make the game work.

Other players' behaviour

You can mute any player at any time. The host can remove a player from the game. To report a player, write to radiationsupport@gmail.com with the date and time of the match, the player's display name and what happened. We do not keep recordings of voice chat, so your description matters.

Keeping and deleting information

  • Lobbies are deleted when the game ends.
  • Vivox keeps voice-service data for a limited time (by default up to 30 days) and does not keep the audio of the chat.
  • Unity keeps your anonymous player ID until it is deleted on request.
  • Files on your computer stay until you delete them: delete the folder named above, or uninstall the game and remove that folder.

To ask us to delete the data linked to your player ID at Unity and Vivox, or for a copy of it, write to radiationsupport@gmail.com with your player ID: open the main menu's "Log folder", open Player.log and copy the ID from the line [UGS] Signed in as …. We pass the request to Unity and answer within 30 days.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example the EU or the UK under the GDPR, or California under the CCPA), you have the right to know what information about you is processed, to get a copy, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object to its use. Write to us to use them. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

Children

Steam accounts are for people aged 13 and over. FalseAlibi is a murder-mystery game for teenagers and adults and is not directed at children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game's online services, write to us and we will have the data deleted.

Security

The game uses no password and stores none. We rely on the security of Steam, Unity and Vivox for what passes through their services, and we keep what the game sends to the minimum it needs to work. Nobody can guarantee perfect security.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts using information differently, we will update this page and its effective date and mention the change in the game's news on Steam.
